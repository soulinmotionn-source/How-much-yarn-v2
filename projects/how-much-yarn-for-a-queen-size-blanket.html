How Much Yarn for a Queen Size Blanket?

A queen-size blanket (about 90″ × 90″) is a big project: expect roughly 4,600 yards of worsted-weight yarn — about 21 skeins of a standard 220-yard skein, with buffer. Most makers choose bulky or super bulky for bed-size blankets to keep the project manageable: about 3,400 yards of bulky or 2,600 yards of super bulky.

For a project this size, buy all your yarn at once from the same dye lot. A queen blanket is also where cost planning matters — at typical prices, yarn alone can run well over $100, so the calculator above helps you budget before you start.

Frequently asked questions

How many skeins of yarn for a queen size blanket?

In worsted weight, about 21 skeins of a standard 220-yard skein (with a 10% buffer). In bulky weight, roughly 28 skeins of 120-yard skeins. In super bulky, about 37 skeins of 70-yard skeins — which is why many makers use jumbo chenille-style yarns sold in large balls for bed blankets.

Is it cheaper to knit or buy a queen blanket?

Handmade is almost never cheaper than store-bought for bed-size blankets — the yarn alone typically costs more than a finished blanket. People make them for the craft, the custom colors, and the quality, not the savings.

What is the best yarn for a large blanket?

Bulky or super bulky acrylic or acrylic-blend yarns are the practical choice: affordable in the large quantities needed, washable, and fast to work. Jumbo chenille yarns are popular for hand-knit (arm-knit) bed blankets.

How long does a queen blanket take to make?

In worsted weight it can take several months of regular crafting. In super bulky or with arm knitting, a motivated maker can finish in a few weeks.
